How to put an Urdu name in Free Fire

Free Fire (FF) does not support Urdu script properly — paste a normal Urdu name and it shows up reversed, with every letter split apart. This guide explains why, and the exact steps to get a properly joined Urdu nickname in the game.

Why Urdu names break in Free Fire

Urdu is written right-to-left, and most of its letters join to their neighbours, changing shape depending on their position in a word — one form at the start, another in the middle, another at the end.

Free Fire renders nicknames left-to-right and does no such shaping. It simply draws the characters in the order they are stored. A normally typed Urdu name is stored in reading order, so the game displays it backwards, and every letter appears in its lonely, isolated form. The name is not wrong — it is stored for the wrong direction.

What you need

  • Your name typed in Urdu (any Urdu keyboard works)
  • The FF Urdu Name converter — free, in your browser
  • For changing an existing nickname: a Name Change Card (from the in-game store, guild rewards or events). Your very first nickname, set at account creation, is free.

Step by step

  1. Open the converter at ffurduname.tsunstudio.cyou.
  2. Type your name normally in the Urdu box, exactly as you would write it anywhere. Gaps between words are fine — they are replaced automatically with the hidden gap Free Fire accepts.
  3. Check the counter. Free Fire allows at most 12 characters, gaps included. The converter counts for you and will not accept a name that is too long — shorten it until it fits.
  4. Tap "Copy nickname". Never retype the converted name by hand; copying keeps the exact letter order the game needs.
  5. In Free Fire, open your profile, tap the edit icon next to your nickname, and paste.
  6. Read the in-game preview before confirming. It should show your name joined and reading correctly. Only then confirm — a rename card is spent once you do.

Good to know

  • The hidden gap. A normal space can break or be rejected in a nickname, so words are joined with a special filler character that looks like a space and Free Fire accepts. The converter inserts it for you.
  • Some gaps are correct Urdu. Letters like alif, dal, re, ze and waw never join to the letter after them. A break after them is how Urdu is written, not a mistake.
  • Vowel marks are removed. Short vowel signs (zabar, zer, pesh) do not belong in nicknames, so the converter strips them.
  • Other apps may show it flipped. The converted name is stored for Free Fire's left-to-right drawing. If a chat app displays it reversed, that app is shaping it its own way — trust the game's preview, not the chat's.

Questions

Why does my Urdu name appear reversed and broken in Free Fire?

Free Fire draws nicknames left-to-right and does not shape Arabic script. Urdu is written right-to-left with letters that join and change form, so a normally pasted Urdu name is stored in reading order and the game displays it reversed, with every letter in its isolated form. A converter fixes this by pre-shaping each letter and storing the name in the visual order the game draws.

How do I write my name in Urdu in Free Fire?

Open the FF Urdu Name converter, type your name normally in Urdu, copy the converted nickname, then in Free Fire open your profile, tap the edit icon next to your nickname, paste the converted name and confirm. Always paste — never retype the converted name — and check the in-game preview before confirming.

What is the nickname character limit in Free Fire?

Free Fire nicknames are limited to 12 characters. In a converted Urdu name, the hidden gap between words also counts as one character. The converter counts letters and gaps together and refuses names that would exceed 12 characters.

Do I need a rename card to change my Free Fire name to Urdu?

Your first nickname, set when you create your account, is free. Changing an existing nickname afterwards requires a Name Change Card, which can be obtained from the in-game store, guild rewards and events. Check the preview carefully before you confirm the change.

Why are some letters in my converted Urdu name not joined?

Some Urdu letters never join to the letter after them — for example alif, dal, re, ze and waw. A small gap after these letters is correct Urdu, not an error. The converter joins every letter that can join and leaves only the gaps that real Urdu has.
